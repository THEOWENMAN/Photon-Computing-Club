We simulatecomputersmade of light.

The Photon Computing Club is a University of Toronto student group that designs optical computers in code. We simulate logic made of laser beams, chips that steer light, and AI hardware that runs on photons. No optics background needed.

01

This is a photon.

A single packet of light. On a photonic chip, it's the signal, the way electrons are the signal in an ordinary processor.

02

It travels in a wire made of light.

A silicon waveguide, about half a micrometre wide, keeps the light trapped and guides it across the chip.

03

Here, it splits in two.

Both halves take different paths. When they meet again, they interfere: adding up, or cancelling out.

04

Interference is math.

Chain enough of these splitters and the light performs matrix multiplication, the core operation of AI, just by passing through.

05

Every colour computes at once.

Each wavelength is its own channel. One chip, many calculations, all at the speed of light.

White light in.
Every colour out.

Today's chips spend much of their energy just moving data around. Light carries it without the heat, sends many colours down one path at once, and does math as it travels. That's why we model it, simulate it and design with it.

Copper vs. light.

Push the data rate and watch the copper wire heat up and blur its signal. Then add colours to the waveguide: each one is another channel, in the same strand.

No resistive heat

Electrons in copper lose energy as heat, and faster switching makes it worse. Light in a waveguide travels with very little loss.

Many colours, one path

Wavelength-division multiplexing sends independent signals down one strand, one per colour.

Math while it moves

Meshes of interferometers multiply a vector by a matrix as light flows through them.

The catch

Light is hard to store and hard to make switch other light. Those open problems are why this field needs new people.
